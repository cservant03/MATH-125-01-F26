    Definition 76. Quota.

    🔗
    🔗
    A weighted voting system with quota \(q\) and player weights \(w_1,w_2,\ldots,w_n\) is written
    \begin{equation*} [q:w_1,w_2,\ldots,w_n]. \end{equation*}
    The quota is the minimum total weight needed for a motion to pass. The notation assigns weight \(w_1\) to player \(P_1\text{,}\) weight \(w_2\) to player \(P_2\text{,}\) and so on.
    🔗

    Subsubsection Limits on the Quota

    Let \(W\) be the total weight of all players. In a meaningful weighted voting system, the quota must satisfy
    \begin{equation*} \frac{W}{2} < q \leq W. \end{equation*}
    The quota must be more than half of the total weight so that a proposal and its opposition cannot both reach the quota. The quota cannot exceed the total weight because otherwise no coalition could ever pass a motion.
    🔗
    When weights and quotas are whole numbers, the smallest majority quota is \(\left\lfloor W/2\right\rfloor+1\text{,}\) and the largest possible quota is \(W\text{.}\) A quota equal to \(W\) requires unanimous support.
    🔗
