    Section Measures of Center and Variation

    Suppose two campus newspapers publish articles with the same average length. One paper might publish articles of about the same length, while the other publishes a mix of short notices and long features. A measure of center describes a typical value. A measure of variation describes how spread out the values are.
    🔗

    Subsection Measures of Center

    Definition 289 Mean.

    The mean is the sum of the observations divided by the number of observations. For a sample of size \(n\text{,}\) the mean is denoted by \(\bar{x}\text{.}\) For a population of size \(N\text{,}\) the mean is denoted by \(\mu\text{.}\)
    \begin{equation*} \bar{x}=\dfrac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i}{n} \qquad \mu=\dfrac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i}{N} \end{equation*}
    Here \(x_i\) denotes an individual observation.
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 290 Median and Mode.

    The median is the center of the ordered observations. Sort the values from smallest to largest. With an odd number of values, take the middle value. With an even number, average the two middle values.
    🔗
    A mode is a value or category with the highest frequency. In these notes, a mode must occur at least twice. Ties at the highest repeated frequency give multiple modes. Mode can summarize categories such as a preferred music genre.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 291 Story Lengths in a Literary Journal.

    Five short stories submitted to a student literary journal are \(8, 10, 10, 12, 15\) pages long. The mean is \(\dfrac{8+10+10+12+15}{5}=11\) pages. The median is \(10\) pages, the third ordered value. The mode is \(10\) pages, which occurs twice.
    🔗
    A sixth submission is \(35\) pages long. The mean increases to \(\dfrac{55+35}{6}=15\) pages. The median increases to \(\dfrac{10+12}{2}=11\) pages. The new value moves the mean by \(4\) pages and the median by \(1\) page.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 292 Mean from a Frequency Table.

    A poetry magazine records how many poems each of ten writers submitted.
    🔗
    Poems submitted Number of writers
    1 3
    2 4
    3 2
    4 1
    Multiply each value by its frequency, add the products, and divide by the total frequency. The mean number of poems per writer is
    \begin{equation*} \dfrac{1\cdot 3+2\cdot 4+3\cdot 2+4\cdot 1}{3+4+2+1}=\dfrac{21}{10}=2.1\text{ poems}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Measures of Variation

    Definition 293 Range.

    The range is the difference between the largest and smallest values.
    \begin{equation*} \text{Range}=\text{maximum}-\text{minimum} \end{equation*}
    The five original stories have a range of \(15-8=7\) pages. With the sixth story, the range is \(35-8=27\) pages.
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 294 Variance and Standard Deviation.

    To find the variance, subtract the mean from each value, square the differences, and add the squares. Divide by \(n-1\) for a sample or by \(N\) for a whole population.
    \begin{equation*} s^2=\dfrac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i-\bar{x})^2}{n-1} \qquad \sigma^2=\dfrac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_i-\mu)^2}{N} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The standard deviation is the square root of the variance. It measures spread around the mean.
    \begin{equation*} s=\sqrt{s^2}\qquad\sigma=\sqrt{\sigma^2} \end{equation*}
    Variance has squared units, and standard deviation has the same units as the data. Standard deviation is zero when all values are equal. Values far from the mean can increase it substantially.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 295 Editing Time at a Campus Radio Station.

    A sample of four radio segments took \(12, 15, 15, 18\) minutes to edit. The mean is \(15\) minutes. The deviations from the mean are \(-3, 0, 0, 3\text{,}\) and their squares sum to \(9+0+0+9=18\text{.}\) The sample variance and sample standard deviation are
    \begin{equation*} s^2=\dfrac{18}{4-1}=6\text{ minutes}^2 \qquad s=\sqrt{6}\approx 2.45\text{ minutes}. \end{equation*}
    If these four observations were the entire population of interest, the variance would be \(\dfrac{18}{4}=4.5\) minutes squared and the standard deviation would be about \(2.12\) minutes.
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 296 Printmaking Supplies.

    Four printmakers used \(5, 6, 6, 7\) sheets of paper in a test run. Find the sample variance and sample standard deviation. Then find the standard deviation if these four printmakers are the entire group of interest.
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 297 Quartiles, Five-Number Summary, and Interquartile Range.

    The quartiles divide the ordered data into four groups of roughly equal size. Write them as \(Q_1\text{,}\) \(Q_2\text{,}\) and \(Q_3\text{,}\) with \(Q_2\) equal to the median. The five-number summary is \((\text{minimum}, Q_1, \text{median}, Q_3, \text{maximum})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The interquartile range, or IQR, measures the spread of the middle half of the ordered data.
    \begin{equation*} \mathrm{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Finding Quartiles by Hand.

    Sort the observations and compute the locator \(L\text{.}\)
     1 
    This locator convention follows Describing Data in Math in Society, credited to David Lippman, Jeff Eldridge, and OnlineStatBook (CC BY-SA).
    \begin{equation*} L=0.25n\text{ for }Q_1\qquad L=0.75n\text{ for }Q_3 \end{equation*}
    • If \(L\) is a whole number, average the values at positions \(L\) and \(L+1\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • If \(L\) is between whole numbers, round up and take the value at that position.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 298 Lengths of Oral History Recordings.

    A campus archive lists these twelve recording lengths in minutes.
    \begin{equation*} 8,\ 11,\ 12,\ 13,\ 14,\ 14,\ 16,\ 17,\ 19,\ 21,\ 23,\ 36 \end{equation*}
    With \(n=12\text{,}\) the locators for \(Q_1\) and \(Q_3\) are \(3\) and \(9\text{.}\)
    \begin{equation*} Q_1=\dfrac{12+13}{2}=12.5 \qquad \text{Median}=\dfrac{14+16}{2}=15 \qquad Q_3=\dfrac{19+21}{2}=20 \end{equation*}
    The five-number summary is \((8, 12.5, 15, 20, 36)\text{.}\) The middle half of the recording lengths spans \(20-12.5=7.5\) minutes, and the full range is \(36-8=28\) minutes.
    🔗
    🔗
    For a roughly symmetric distribution with no pronounced outliers, the mean and standard deviation summarize center and spread. For a strongly skewed distribution or one with pronounced outliers, the median and IQR are often more representative because they depend on positions in the ordered data.
    🔗
