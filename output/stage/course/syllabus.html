    Subsection Course Description

    A liberal arts course providing an introduction to logical inference, statistics, probability, management science, social choice, concepts of size and shape, algorithmic processes and consumer mathematics. Emphasis on understanding rather than computational expertise.

    Subsection Course Outcomes

    This General Education course introduces fundamental algebraic terminology and skills; demonstrates how ingenuity and creativity are essential in developing mathematical models to solve real world problems; cultivates critical thinking and logical reasoning for effective problem solving and rational decision-making in a complex technological society; and develops communication skills for translating real world situations into symbolic mathematical relationships and for interpreting mathematical results within their real world contexts.

    Subsection Evaluatation

    Homework
    🔗
    Each learning outcome in the course has one 10-point homework assignment in MyOpenMath. These assignments provide practice applying the concepts and skills associated with each outcome and help students prepare for quizzes and lab reports. A student’s Homework score is the total number of points earned across all MyOpenMath homework assignments.
    🔗
    Quizzes
    🔗
    Quizzes are administered approximately every fourth class meeting throughout most of the semester. Quizzes assess a student’s ability to independently apply the concepts and skills developed through class activities, worksheets, and assigned course materials.
    🔗
    Each quiz is scored on a 0-3 scale:
    🔗
    3:
    🔗
    Demonstrates a strong understanding of the outcome with little to no error. Work is complete, mathematically correct, and clearly communicates the solution process.
    🔗
    2:
    🔗
    Demonstrates substantial understanding of the outcome but contains one or more minor errors. The overall solution process is appropriate and most of the work is correct.
    🔗
    1:
    🔗
    Demonstrates partial understanding of the outcome but contains significant errors or omissions. Some correct work is shown, but the student has not yet demonstrated competency.
    🔗
    0:
    🔗
    The outcome is not attempted or the work submitted does not demonstrate an understanding of the outcome.
    🔗
    Each quiz includes two in-class attempts. For each quiz, the highest score earned across all attempts is used when calculating the Quiz portion of the course grade.
    🔗
    A third attempt is available only after attending tutoring. Students must work with a tutor to review the relevant worksheet problems and their first two attempts before a third attempt will be granted.
    🔗
    Quizzes are not given as make-ups. The multiple-attempt structure is intended to accommodate absences and scheduling conflicts. Students who miss an in-class attempt may use a remaining attempt to demonstrate competency.
    🔗
    For the final quizzes of the course, only one in-class attempt may be provided during the final exam period. Additional attempts must be completed outside of class time by appointment.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Grading

    Your final course grade will be determined by your performance in two categories: Homework and Quizzes. A letter grade will be determined separately for each category using the requirements below. Your final course grade will be the lowest of the two category grades.
    🔗
    Letter Grade Homework Points Quiz Points
    A 180-210 51-63
    B 160-179 42-50
    C 140-159 33-41
    D 120-139 24-32
    F 0-119 0-23
    Here are some examples showing how final course grades are determined:
    🔗
    1. A student earns \(190\) Homework Points and \(56\) Quiz Points. Both totals correspond to an A, so the student earns an A in the course.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. A student earns \(188\) Homework Points and \(47\) Quiz Points. The Homework Points correspond to an A, while the Quiz Points correspond to a B. Since the lower of the two grades is a B, the student earns a B in the course.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. A student earns \(152\) Homework Points and \(54\) Quiz Points. The Homework Points correspond to a C, while the Quiz Points correspond to an A. Since the lower of the two grades is a C, the student earns a C in the course.
      🔗

    Subsection Academic Integrity Policy

    In MATH 132B, any occurrence of cheating or academic dishonesty may be officially documented. The student or students involved may then receive a copy of the documentation. The first occurrence may result in failure of the coursework and will be reported to the Office of Student Conduct Programs for the record. Any additional occurrence will be reported to the Office of Student Conduct Programs for adjudication.
    🔗
    You may work in groups or with tutors on homework and lab reports when permitted, but you must complete your own work. Quizzes must be completed independently without resources. Inappropriate use of laptops, cell phones, calculators, tablets, websites, outside assistance, or other resources is considered cheating. If you are unsure whether something is permitted, ask your instructor before using it.
    🔗
    See SVSU’s Academic Integrity Policy for additional information.
