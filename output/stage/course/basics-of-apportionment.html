    Section Basics of Apportionment

    Outcomes

    1. Explain the apportionment problem and its basic requirements.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Calculate the standard divisor and standard quotas.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Identify the lower and upper quota for each group.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Determine whether an allocation satisfies the quota rule.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Apportionment is the problem of dividing a fixed number of indivisible items among groups of different sizes. The items might be legislative seats, tutors, employees, vehicles, or pieces of equipment. The goal is to make each group’s share of the items approximately proportional to its share of the population or need.
    🔗

    Subsection The Apportionment Problem

    Definition 101. Apportionment.

    🔗
    🔗
    Exact proportionality is often impossible because the ideal shares are usually decimals while the items must be assigned in whole numbers. Apportionment methods provide systematic ways to decide where those unavoidable rounding differences should occur.
    🔗

    🔗
    The standard quota is the group’s ideal fractional share of the available items. The standard quotas add to the total number of items being apportioned, apart from small differences caused by rounding displayed decimals.
